## Findings, ranked by impact
**B4-01 A download's event is posted with `View.post`, which the view's own rule forbids.** After a renderer crash `owner.webView` is the destroyed, detached WebView, so `core.download` is lost while the view waits to recover. While the screen is covered (the view detached) the event is delayed. `owner.webView` is also read on the IO thread. Evidence: K/requests/Downloads.kt:94,106; the rule is at K/MinimalWebView.kt:123-125; the dead WebView is at :362-368,382-383. Q4. **simplify**: `owner.mainHandler.post {`. S. Confidence high on the code; the loss follows from `View.post` on a view that is never attached again, and was not tried on a device. Breaks nothing.

**B4-02 The recovery window is written once, at `recovering`, and four entry points miss it.** (a) `hook` rejects `hook_not_handled`, not the `unavailable` the docs promise, because `processGone` emptied the contexts (= A4-02). (b) Re-enabling a key in `hookKeys` while the view waits warns "no hook attaches for it", yet `recover` attaches it (:142-143). (c) `navigate` back, forward and stop call the destroyed WebView. (d) `install()` removes the dead WebView's ScriptHandlers. Evidence: K/MinimalWebViewModule.kt:127-130,148-150; K/MinimalWebView.kt:279,289-293,382-383; K/MinimalWebViewScripts.kt:71; docs/props.md:31-33; T/types.ts:256-257. Q4. **simplify**: reject `unavailable` first in `hook`; warn only when `key !in hookKeys`; return early from `navigate` while recovering; `scriptHandlers.clear()` at the top of `install()`; one line at the `webView` declaration (:45-49) saying it is dead while recovering. S. Confidence high for (a) and (b); (c) and (d) rely on Chromium's destroyed-WebView checks, which were not checked. Breaks nothing (no suite calls these during a rebuild).

**B4-03 `ref.hook` has no way to settle.** A handler that never calls `reply`, or whose WebView crashes (Android) or closes before it does, leaves the JS promise pending forever. Every other wait that crosses layers settles at the Document's end or at teardown: asks (K/MinimalWebView.kt:345), `evaluate` (K/MinimalWebViewScripts.kt:129-133, S/MinimalWebView+Scripts.swift:48-52), the bridge (T/bridge/createBridge.ts:119-135). Evidence: only the reply closure holds the promise (K/MinimalWebViewModule.kt:148-156, S/MinimalWebViewModule.swift:58-69); the contexts are dropped without rejecting anything (K/MinimalWebView.kt:279, S/MinimalWebView.swift:261); docs/native-hooks.md:55-57 doesn't say. Q4. **document** (S: "a call whose hook detaches before replying never settles"), or simplify (M: each context keeps its in-flight promises and rejects them `unavailable` at detach, about 10 lines per platform; the maintainer picks). Confidence high. Breaks: nothing (the only change is new rejections).

**B4-04 A hook's `ask` made after the view closed is never answered.** The contract says null "when the view or its page goes away". Kotlin's context holds the view strongly, so after `destroy()` (which failed the pending asks once, :413) a new `ask` registers with nothing left to cancel it, and it also keeps the destroyed view alive. Swift's `ask` registers between `invalidate` and `deinit`, and `deinit`'s `tearDown` returns early. Evidence: K/Hooks.kt:87,107,121-122; K/MinimalWebView.kt:303-312; S/Hooks.swift:76,106-108; S/MinimalWebView.swift:253-254,486-488; docs/native-hooks.md:54,70-71. Q4. **simplify**: `if (destroyed) return onAnswer(null)` in Kotlin `ask`; `guard webView != nil else { return onAnswer(nil) }` in Swift `ask`. S. Confidence high. Breaks nothing.
